Experience

I've spent 30 years making games and playful things: Grand Theft Auto at DMA Design, mobile before smartphones, interactive TV before app stores, then browser, social and whatever came next.

I've shipped 100+ games, led studios' product and production work, built an R&D centre and co-written a book about game making in the 21st century. The thread through all of it is helping people make something worth playing, and giving it a chance to find its audience

How I work

At Denki we called the role Product Architect: shaping what a game becomes and building the process that gets it there. I like being close enough to the work to prototype an idea, question a feature or play through the latest build.

I translate a founder's intent into priorities a team can act on, test assumptions early and keep scope, quality and commercial goals in view. I've spent more than 20 years managing distributed teams and taking responsibility for budgets, capacity and delivery.

Producer / Product Architect | Denki

2006-2015

Led development and live operations across mobile, interactive TV, browser and console at a studio recognised as UK pioneers of casual gaming.

Interactive television. We shipped playable games to Sky and DirecTV when the living room had no app store, no storefront and no second screen. Discovery was an electronic programme guide, the input device was a remote control, and the release date was a broadcast slot that did not move. It is the same problem connected TV is solving again now with cloud streaming and phones as controllers, and most of what we learned about designing for ten feet and a thumb still holds.

The production model. 100+ titles in nine years, against fixed budgets, licensor approvals, platform certification and immovable external deadlines. That volume was only possible because we built the system for it: a bespoke production model, a talent pipeline and a release cadence designed around the constraints rather than fighting them.

The games. I produced Quarrel, an award-winning original game, from vertical slice through release on iOS and Xbox 360, scaling the team and establishing the studio's original-IP process. Other work included Denki Blocks! and Save the Day!, alongside licensed games for Shrek, Moshi Monsters, Ben 10, WALL-E and Scooby-Doo, for partners including the BBC, DreamWorks, Fox, LEGO, Mattel, Mind Candy, Nickelodeon and Pixar.
