01The bet

The goal: tiny teams making playthings to a Denki standard in hours, not weeks, with agents filling the gaps.

Denki solved creative assembly lines 20 years ago. A team of three (Product Architect, Coder and Artist), working inside a network of context, standards and process, took six to ten weeks to make a Denki-quality product for interactive television.

My bet is that with an agentic exoskeleton I can make a Denki-quality plaything in hours, inside one coherent, consistent production process: Creative Assembly Loops.

The Product Architect role (scout, shaper, showrunner, editor, storyteller) is the part agents can't do, and it's the part I've done for 30 years.

All times in hours and minutes. Seed to ship is elapsed time on the clock; human time is my share of it, the rest is Claude's. Remixes and ports run from kick-off, because they reuse a shipped game's design. ~ marks an estimate from timestamps.

Success signals

  • Seed-to-ship time falls loop over loop, even as modules add complexity. Too early. One full loop has been timed so far (002 Rondo, 1h 42m). Remixes and ports aren't like-for-like.
  • Amp! holds its own in a blind comparison with the sealed original Denki prototype. Not yet run. Amp! is up next.
  • My hands-on time shrinks loop over loop while my judgement carries further: fewer rounds to each sign-off, and nothing signed off gets rebuilt. Early sign. Human time was 1h 02m on the last full loop, and under 35m on the remix and the port. Nothing signed off on 002 Rondo had to be rebuilt.

Failure signals

  • Five loops in, ship times aren't falling.
  • Five loops in, quality is dropping as speed rises or as modules are added.
  • Five loops in, my hands-on time isn't shrinking, or it is but my judgement isn't carrying: sign-offs take more rounds, or signed-off work gets rebuilt.

If any of these fire, I'll change the experiment rather than push harder.

03How it works

A human-AI co-production loop for petit playthings: tiny, instantly playable browser games. Small enough to make in an afternoon, finished enough to play on your phone, satisfying enough to snack on. Each loop ships one thin slice, a polished first playable, and leaves the process better than it found it.

04Why

Making is cheap now, so the constraint has moved: choosing what to make, proving it early and polishing it properly. Denki solved that 20 years ago with process, not headcount: creative assembly lines that took games from idea to live on Sky in six to ten weeks (TV as a Games Platform). Agents let a tiny team run that process again as augmentation, not automation, in loops that get better with every pass.

The full argument: Creative Assembly Loops (Substack).

05Roadmap

For now, I'm focused on Version 1, Creative Assembly Loops: one loop, with a layer added for every plaything it ships, and plenty to do inside it. V2 and V3 are where one loop becomes a network of loops.
