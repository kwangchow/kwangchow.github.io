Who designed and delivered it

Sean Taylor, Director of InGAME

  • Headhunted from industry to set it up, after two decades designing and producing games, from the culture-defining Grand Theft Auto at DMA Design to BAFTA-winning Quarrel at Denki.
  • Built it from scratch. Led it for six years. Owned the strategy, budget and operating model. Designed the programmes, selected the cohorts and built the evaluation. Led a team of 12 with a wider bench of industry and academic partners, reporting to a steering board of industry, public-sector and academic leaders.
  • Designed a model others built on. Responsive R&D: research rigour at industry speed, delivering impact far beyond its targets. Its evidence underpins Scotland's National Strategy for Gaming (2026) and the business case for CoSTAR Realtime Lab, part of a £75.6m national research and development infrastructure network.

What it was

A small bet on a famous cluster

Dundee gave the world Grand Theft Auto and Lemmings. In 2018 the Arts and Humanities Research Council backed InGAME to grow that cluster through collaborative, responsive R&D, with industry partners including Microsoft, Sony PlayStation and Ukie.

How it worked

Tailored support, not a fixed scheme

InGAME didn't deliver a programme; it built an operating model.
It met each company where it was, from first idea to scaling studio, and delivered responsive R&D support to accelerate its growth potential.

De-risk

Creative experimentation

What transfers

Five lessons for a gaming accelerator

  1. 1

    Design for impact; work backwards.

    Set outcomes and success measures first, then build delivery and evaluation around them.

    Every target beaten
  2. 2

    Sector expertise sharpens selection.

    Use industry knowledge and trusted networks to find promising teams and judge commercial potential.

    177 projects
    93% would return
  3. 3

    Tailor each journey to the venture.

    Match expertise, resources and mentoring to each founder's stage, ambition and next milestone.

    One programme,
    four pathways to growth
  4. 4

    Turn connections into commercial opportunity.

    Broker partnerships that open markets and unlock capital.

    SmARtview
    £250k Innovate UK follow-on funding
  5. 5

    Deliver incrementally; learn continuously.

    Short feedback loops and shared learning, so the programme improves while it's running.

    Open innovation toolkit
